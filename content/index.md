## About

I'm Principal Architect, AI Engineering at [NetSPI](https://www.netspi.com/), where I design and build AI applications and the infrastructure underneath them.

I started out doing AI research in the private sector, then got into security and spent a few years on secure code review.
Now I'm back in AI.
Outside of work I'm really into endurance sports, especially triathlons and the Ironman circuit.
I built [Fyt](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fyt-all-in-one-training/id6789987104), an iOS app for that training.
It keeps swimming, cycling, running and strength work in one journal, so you can plan a week, log the sessions and see what's changing.
Pushing the human body has a lot in common with pushing innovation: you keep testing where the limit is, and with enough work it moves.
I'm a little obsessed with doing that in both.

Most of my work is about measuring what AI systems can actually do.
I built [EchoBench](https://www.netspi.com/blog/technical-blog/ai-ml-pentesting/introducing-echobench-a-human-calibrated-benchmark-for-autonomous-pentesting/), a benchmark that scores autonomous pentesting agents against a measured cohort of human testers, and I write about AI security on the [NetSPI blog](https://www.netspi.com/authors/jake-scheetz/).

This site is for longer notes on fundamental AI research: how models learn, how we measure them, and where they break.
